**Android only: 0.12.1 → 0.13.0.** The Mac app stays at 0.12.1, and nothing about how the two talk to each other changed, so either keeps working next to the other's older version. The Android app updates itself: you'll get the usual **"Update ready — tap to install"** notification.

## Send diagnostics

When something doesn't work, **Settings ▸ Support ▸ Send diagnostics** builds a report **on your phone** and shows you all of it before anything leaves:

- the app version, your phone model and Android version,
- the state of the link to your Mac and of each permission,
- **why Android last ended the app** (low memory, closed by you, crash, not responding, …), which is the usual cause of "it stopped syncing by itself",
- the last lines of the app's and the daemon's logs, with your data removed.

**Share report** opens Android's share sheet, so you pick where it goes. **GitHub issue** opens a pre-filled issue form with a short summary; attach the full report as a file after you've read it. **GitHub issues are public.** Nothing is sent automatically, there is no telemetry, and the report is deliberately not copied to the clipboard (the app's own clipboard sync would forward it to your Mac).

## Your data stays out of the logs

The old **Logs ▸ Share** passed log lines on as they were, and those lines could include the start of what you had copied, file names, your relay's address and your Mac's IP. That is fixed in two layers:

- The app and the daemon now **log lengths, counts and states**, not clipboard text, file names, addresses or package names.
- **Logs, Daemon Log and the report** also remove them again before showing or sharing anything: clipboard text, file names, addresses and IPs, your Mac's and phone's names, package names, content paths and the pairing room and key. Debug builds still show the raw log on screen so a developer can read their own; **Share is always cleaned.**

This cleaning is best-effort: it recognises addresses, paths, package names and your own device names, but cannot recognise an arbitrary word. That's why you read the report before you share it.

## Known limitations

- **"Recent exits" needs this version.** Android only keeps that record for the app that asks, so the section starts filling in after you update; an older APK shows "Not available in this build".
- **The daemon log is read the way it always was.** If the daemon is down and Wireless debugging is off, the report says the daemon log could not be read instead of waiting forever (it gives up after 15 s).
- **Updating is the usual update.** The app downloads this APK in the background and you confirm the install in Android's own installer; your pairing and settings are kept. It is a new version rather than a quiet update because native code changed.
